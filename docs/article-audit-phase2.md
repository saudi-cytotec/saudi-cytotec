# Article Audit — Phase 2

## Objective
Differentiate the existing article catalog by editorial value rather than by producing more URLs.

## Findings
The current article builder already varies paragraphs and headings by article, but many pages still share the same structural rhythm. Google states that large-scale content can be problematic when many pages provide little added value, including pages generated at scale without meaningful user value.

## Priority editorial groups

### Group A — medication uses
- approved-medical-uses-misoprostol
- not-all-uses-are-alike
- off-label-use-meaning
- misoprostol-in-clinical-references

Editorial distinction:
- approved uses: regulatory framing and source hierarchy
- not-all-uses-are-alike: compare source types and contexts
- off-label-use-meaning: define the professional meaning of off-label use
- clinical references: explain how professional references should be read

### Group B — safety and side effects
- general-safety-warnings
- common-side-effects
- mild-symptoms-not-always-safe
- when-symptoms-are-emergencies
- rare-serious-complications

Editorial distinction:
- safety warnings: contraindication and warning architecture
- common side effects: expected-vs-concerning framework
- mild symptoms: why severity can change over time
- emergencies: triage-oriented warning signs
- rare complications: why low frequency does not remove urgency

### Group C — pregnancy
- warning-signs-in-pregnancy
- bleeding-in-early-pregnancy
- pregnancy-and-medicines-faq
- education-vs-individual-treatment
- pregnancy-follow-up-care

Editorial distinction:
- warning signs: symptom combinations and urgency
- bleeding: differential causes and limits of online interpretation
- medicines FAQ: common questions without turning answers into treatment instructions
- education vs treatment: boundary of general information
- follow-up: continuity of care and why improvement does not always end follow-up

### Group D — evidence
- how-to-evaluate-medical-evidence
- clinical-studies-vs-anecdotes
- reading-a-paper-as-non-specialist
- limits-of-online-medical-info
- why-medical-recommendations-change

Editorial distinction:
- evidence hierarchy
- anecdote vs study
- reading methodology
- limitations of online information
- why recommendations evolve

## Phase 2 rules
1. Do not add new articles merely to target more keywords.
2. Give each priority article one clearly different editorial question.
3. Add original explanatory value, not synonym variations.
4. Strengthen internal links only where the destination answers a distinct next question.
5. Keep medical content educational and avoid dosing, regimens, purchasing routes, or self-treatment instructions.
6. Re-check title, description, headings, related links, and structured data after each content batch.

## Next implementation batch
Rewrite/differentiate the first priority group (medication uses), then validate overlap before moving to safety, pregnancy, and evidence groups.
